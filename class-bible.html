Twenty-one classes, twenty-one hands

Six colors, closed. Every class gets a character with something to want, and a distinct artistic tradition to be drawn in — so a player can name the class from across the table without reading a word. All twenty-one traditions are settled and ready to generate against. They stay settled until the images themselves argue otherwise — a style changes on evidence from a real render, not on a second guess.

The roster closes at 21

Six colors yield six pure classes and fifteen pairs — and every one of the fifteen is already in the list, with no duplicates and nothing missing. The wheel is whole and it is closed.

Color identity, in full

The wheel says which classes exist. This says what the colors are — the element each one argues for, the character it is cost in, and the hex it prints at. Every class is a color doubled or a pair of colors, and that one fact drives its mana ladder, its power budget and the gradient across its card face.

What holds 21 art styles together

Giving every class its own artistic tradition is the bold move here, and it will read beautifully — or fall apart into 21 unrelated games. It only works if a hard set of constants runs underneath every style. Vary the hand; never vary the frame. The constants below are targets, not intentions — every one of them is checkable on a finished image.

  • One value structure — mid-key. Across all twenty-one styles: keep near-black (below value 32) under about 2% of pixels, hold roughly two-thirds of the image in the 64–159 midrange, and land mean luminance near 113/255. The subject reads as the dark shape against a lit ground. This is the single constant most likely to be violated by an image generator, which defaults to dramatic low-key lighting — check it numerically, don't eyeball it.
  • One crop discipline. Same aspect ratio, same safe margins, subject occupying the same portion of frame per card type. A Follower reads as a Follower in all 21 styles.
  • Color obeys the class, not the style. Each tradition gets adapted into that class's two hues. A cave painting for the Shaman still resolves red-and-green; a sumi-e for the Shapeshifter still resolves green-and-blue. The card face itself is painted as a left-to-right gradient between the class’s two hues — the art has to sit on that without fighting it.
  • Edges are shared. One border treatment, one nameplate, one type-line across the whole game. All the variety lives inside the art box.
  • Saturation ceiling — lower than instinct says. No class reaches pure black or a fully saturated primary. Warm-neutral mid-tones carry the image and the oxides do the work, so the few bright notes read as light rather than as color. Every class holds that restraint in its own hue family. Letting some classes run hot and some run muted is the fastest way to make 21 styles look like 21 games.
  • Twenty-nine images per class. Eight followers, eight minions, eight spells, and five singles — hero, champion, ultimate, weapon, armor. Across the full roster that is 609 images. Worth knowing before commissioning.
  • One aspect ratio, fixed before the first generation run. Decide the ratio and the safe margins up front; everything generated after that is reusable and everything before it isn’t.

The six pure classes

These are the anchors — each one is the undiluted argument for its color, and its art style should be the most classical reading of that element. Four of these heroes were named long before this document and keep their names.

01-pyromancer

Caine

Pyromancer · 🔴🔴 Flame 🔥 Locked

An arsonist-scholar who holds that fire is the only honest element — it never negotiates, never persists, never pretends to be something else. Caine burns his own spellbook page by page on the theory that memorized magic is dead magic, which is charming right up until you realize he means it about people too. Twitchy, badly scarred, treating the scars as decoration. His card text already says it: he discards to draw. He is always spending something he can't get back, and he is always delighted about it. Joy is the emotional register for this class, and it is the note to hold across every card. Caine is laughing in most of his cards. Not smirking, not grim: openly, inappropriately laughing.

Art traditionPolished digital illustration, fire-lit and mid-key

Medium
Smooth, blended digital painting — rendered and illustrative, with no visible paint texture. Detail concentrates in the face and falls off outward; the flame stays loose and gestural while the face is the only fully resolved thing in frame.
Light
Fire is the only light source and it sits inside the frame, behind and beside the subject. Rim-light along hair, shoulder and forearm; the subject's front stays in warm shadow.
Value
Mid-key, not low-key. Near-black is all but absent and the bulk of the image holds the midrange. The figure is a dark shape in a hot bright field — never a lit shape in darkness. At thumbnail it reads as dark silhouette on glowing ground.
Composition
Three-quarter to full figure with air around it. Dynamic diagonal, weight off-center, arms open, caught mid-gesture. No environment whatsoever — flame and smoke are the entire background and all depth comes from value.
Signature
The flame is becoming something. A phoenix head resolves out of the fire behind the subject's shoulder, at low contrast — visible only on a second look. Every Pyromancer card carries one of these.
Edges
Lost-and-found. Hair and shoulders dissolve into the flame; the silhouette is never fully closed.
Palette
No pure black and no saturated cadmium red. The dominant notes are warm-neutral browns and oxide reds; that restraint is what lets the few bright spots read as heat.
02-chronomancer

Vesper

Chronomancer · 🟡🟡 Time ⏳ Locked

Not a wizard who stops time — one who has already lived this battle and is bored of it. Vesper speaks in the past tense about things that haven't happened, and is ageless in a subtly wrong way: too smooth, too still, blinking out of rhythm with everyone else. Her tragedy is that she can see every branch and is therefore never surprised and never delighted. She fights not to win but to find an outcome she hasn't already sat through. The one villain-coded hero who isn't a villain.

Art traditionCopperplate etching with chromatic ghosting

Medium
Fine engraved line and crosshatch modeling — 18th-century scientific plate, no painterly texture anywhere.
Light
Flat, even, sourceless. Form built entirely by hatch density.
Composition
Each figure printed three times in slight registration offset — one cyan-shifted behind, one amber-shifted ahead, the true figure sharp between them.
Signature
The only class on a pale ground. Against the rest of the set it reads as a page from a different, older book.
Palette
Plate white · sepia ink · registration cyan · registration amber
03-druid

Briar

Druid · 🟢🟢 Growth 🌿 Locked

Not a gentle nature-lover — a combatant on the forest's side of a war humans didn't notice starting. Briar is patient to the point of cruelty: she will let a wound fester if the rot feeds something downstream. Bark-scarred forearms, seed pouches, a voice like a slow tree. She measures time in seasons and finds urgency vulgar, which makes her genuinely difficult to be around and completely impossible to rush.

Art traditionArt Nouveau botanical plate

Medium
Flat decorative color under a heavy confident contour line. Gold-leaf ornament crowding inward from the frame edge.
Light
None — this style is decorative, not illuminated. Depth comes from layering and line weight.
Composition
Symmetrical, mandorla-framed, subject centered and static. Deliberately the stillest cards in the game.
Signature
Ornament density scales with the card's cost. Cheap cards are nearly bare; expensive ones are overgrown, the border eating into the art.
Palette
Sap green · ochre · dusty rose · leaf gold
04-sorceress

Crystal

Sorceress · 🔵🔵 Frost ❄️ Locked

Not cold-hearted — the opposite. Crystal froze the world around her to stop herself feeling it, and it worked, which is the tragedy. Aristocratic bearing, surgical diction, profoundly lonely. Everything she touches lasts forever whether it wanted to or not; her purest expression of love is preservation, and nobody she has ever loved has enjoyed receiving it. Mechanically that is the frost mage's whole thesis and it lines up exactly: she does not kill you, she stops you. Slow, root, freeze, deny — the opponent is rarely destroyed so much as never permitted to act, and control is just preservation aimed at someone else.

Modeled on: World of Warcraft frost mage. Her eight effects are already that kit verbatim — Frost Bolt, Ice Lance, Snap, Frost Nova, Ice Block, Blizzard, Endless Winter, Frostbite. Her minions are all cold-climate beasts; the one iconic frost-mage summon missing is a water elemental, which is worth adding as her signature champion.

Art traditionSilverpoint on prepared blue-grey paper

Medium
Silverpoint line with drybrush white gouache highlights. Almost no color anywhere — the most restrained hand in the set.
Light
Cold, diffuse, directionless, like an overcast snowfield. No shadow has an edge.
Composition
Extreme negative space. The subject occupies roughly a third of the frame; the rest is empty pale field. Small figure, enormous silence.
Structure
Hard crystalline geometry is the one thing drawn with conviction — faceted ice growing in straight runs and sharp terminations, cutting across all that soft grey. Everything organic is barely indicated; everything frozen is exact.
Signature
Everything is almost not there, except one saturated cold glow at the point of casting. Where the Pyromancer is a dark figure in a hot bright field, the Sorceress inverts it exactly: a pale figure in a pale field with a single blue note. She is the quiet card you lean in to see — and the deliberate opposite pole from the locked class, which is what makes her the right second lock.
Palette
Paper blue-grey · graphite · lead white · one saturated glacial blue
05-necromancer

Viol

Necromancer · 🟣🟣 Soul 🪦 Locked

A grief-worker, not a villain. Viol raises the dead because he refuses the premise that anyone should simply be gone, and he keeps a written ledger of every soul he has spent doing it. Soft-spoken, apologetic, entirely unstoppable. He looks tired in every single card — that's the note to hold across the whole class. He is not enjoying this and he is not going to stop.

Art traditionDutch vanitas in charcoal and bone black

Medium
Dry media — charcoal, bone black, chalk — smudged with the hand. Leave the fingerprints in.
Light
Hard chiaroscuro from a single candle just outside frame. Deep, swallowing shadow across two-thirds of every image.
Composition
Still-life logic even in action shots: objects arranged, not caught. Everything looks posed for a portrait nobody survived.
Signature
A memento mori tucked in every frame — guttering candle, a fly, a tipped glass, a stopped watch. Never the focus, always present.
Palette
Bone white · bitumen brown · violet-black · muted gold
06-warrior

Hale

Warrior · ⚪⚪ Durability 🛡️ Locked

The only hero in the game with no magic and no interest in acquiring any. Hale's entire thesis is that a person who refuses to fall beats a person who can do anything once. Dry, unimpressed, and startlingly kind to whoever is standing behind them. Their armor is old and has been repaired many times, and not one of the repairs has been hidden.

Art traditionCarved stone relief

Medium
Shallow bas-relief chiseled into weathered limestone. Tool marks, chip damage, and lichen at the edges.
Light
Hard raking sidelight — the only thing creating form is the shadow cast by physical depth.
Composition
Friezelike and frontal. Figures in profile or full-face, arranged along a baseline like a procession.
Signature
Zero luminous effects anywhere in the class. No glow, no particles, no magic. In a game where everything else radiates, the Warrior is the one thing that's just an object — and that's the whole read.
Palette
Limestone buff · iron-oxide stain · verdigris · shadow grey

The fifteen blends

Each dual class should feel like it needed both parents — not a compromise between them but a third thing neither could reach alone. The art tradition blends the same way: pick a medium that sits genuinely between the two pure styles rather than splitting the difference in color only.

07-wizard

Arc

Wizard · 🔴🟡 Plasma 💥 Locked

Fire without the honesty, arcane without the patience. Arc discovered that if you push flame past the point where it is still fire, you get something with no name and no rules, and he has been pushing ever since. Manic, over-caffeinated, speaks in incomplete equations and finishes none of them because he is already testing the next thing. Permanently missing one eyebrow. The spell-heavy card mix is his character rather than a stat line: Arc does not delegate. He fields almost no creatures because he has never once trusted a problem to anything he didn't personally detonate. Every other class brings an army; he brings a handful of things he is about to do himself.

Card mix: spell-dense, creature-light — the most spells and the fewest bodies in the game.

Art traditionLong-exposure light painting on a lab plate

Medium
Photographic, not painted. Long-exposure light trails with genuine lens artifacts — bloom, flare, magenta fringing at the highlight edges.
Light
The subject is the light source and it is overexposed. Blown-out blue-white core losing all detail.
Composition
Centered and radial, set against a gridded scientific plate with handwritten annotations and exposure data in the margins.
Signature
Motion trails that record where the subject was a second ago. Nothing in this class is ever still.
Palette
Plate black · electric blue-white · magenta fringe · arc orange
08-shaman

Ash

Shaman · 🔴🟢 Magma 🌋 Locked

Fire and growth are the same cycle observed at different points. Ash tends volcanic soil and understands that the mountain isn't angry, it's fertile — the eruption and the orchard are one process with a gap in the middle. Ritual-minded, unhurried, treats catastrophe as a season that will pass. The name works twice, which is the point: ash is what the volcano leaves and ash is a tree.

Art traditionOchre-and-charcoal cave painting

Medium
Ritual pigment applied to rough rock — red ochre, charcoal, white clay, blown and hand-pressed rather than brushed.
Light
Flickering and warm, as if lit by a torch held at arm's length. Deep pitting in the stone catches real shadow.
Composition
Simplified powerful animal forms, overlapping without perspective. Hand stencils in the margins.
Signature
Deliberately the most primitive-looking cards in the game. The rock's grain and irregularity show through every mark — the surface is half the image.
Palette
Red ochre · charcoal · white clay · basalt
09-trinket-mage

Quench

Trinket-mage · 🔴🔵 Charge 🫧 Locked

Cannot cast a single spell directly — everything Quench does runs through a device, and every device works the same way: put fire and ice in the same small box and contain the argument. An artificer in a dream-punk register: brass, pressure gauges, condensation beading on cold pipe, steam under genuine load — machines built to a plausible engineering logic that then do something no engine could. Tinkerer's optimism, singed fingertips, pockets full of prototypes that mostly work. He is item-heavy because his power is inventory, not talent: take the contraptions away and there is no wizard underneath, and he knows it, and it doesn't bother him nearly as much as it should.

Card mix: item-dense — the most equipment, weapon and armor cards in the game. Aesthetic: dream-punk artificer, steam-powered.

Art traditionExploded-view patent plate, brass and blueprint

Medium
Isometric technical illustration on aged drafting paper — parts floating apart along thin leader lines with numbered callouts — but the metal is rendered as real material: brass, copper, cold-sweating steel.
Light
Schematic flatness for the linework, with genuine specular hits on the brass. The drawing is a document; the machine in it is an object.
Composition
Blueprint ground with white line and a second annotation layer in heat-orange marking where fire meets frost. Condensation and escaping steam are drawn with the same technical precision as the parts.
Signature
Dream-punk, not steampunk: every diagram is plausible right up until it isn't. The tolerances are believable, the callouts are numbered, and the machine described could not possibly work. Precise, warm, and slightly absurd given what the things do.
Palette
Drafting blue · brass · copper · white line · heat orange
10-warlock

Brand

Warlock · 🔴🟣 Debt ☄️ Locked

Made a deal, then made a second deal to cover the first, and is now several layers deep in an arrangement he no longer fully understands. Charming in a way that is visibly load-bearing. Fire and shadow both take payment in advance, and Brand is running out of things to sell. Funny, generous, and doing arithmetic behind his eyes the entire time you're talking to him.

Art traditionScorched illuminated manuscript

Medium
Blackletter marginalia and gold rubrication on vellum — then burned. Charred edges, holes eaten through the page, the illustration continuing around the damage.
Light
Flat medieval illumination with no modeling, undercut by real scorch shadow where the page has curled.
Composition
Text-block logic: the figure occupies an initial capital or a margin, with grotesques and small demons crawling the border.
Signature
Annotations in a second, later hand — red ink corrections by someone who read this after Brand did and was alarmed.
Palette
Vellum cream · iron-gall black · minium red · gold · char
11-demon-hunter

Kell

Demon Hunter · 🔴⚪ Fury 🔺 Locked

Took the enemy's power in order to fight the enemy, and is now on a clock nobody else can see. Kell is fast, physical, and has traded something irreplaceable for it — the deal is already done and there is no version of this that ends well. Grim, unexpectedly funny, refuses to plan past the current month because planning implies there'll be one after.

Art traditionUkiyo-e woodblock with horror hatching

Medium
Woodblock print — flat color fills with visible grain, hard spot-blacks, and dense hatching borrowed from horror manga where the shadow gets bad.
Light
No gradient. Light is a shape you cut, not a falloff you render.
Composition
Aggressively diagonal and off-balance, with kinetic speed-lines and impact bursts breaking the frame edge.
Signature
Every composition is mid-motion and slightly unstable — the class that never has its feet under it.
Palette
Indigo · bone · vermilion · block black
12-windrunner

Gale

Windrunner · 🟡🟢 Terrain 🌎 Locked

Not a courier — a protector, and the flying is incidental to that. What matters is that Gale puts herself between people and the thing coming for them, and that she has said so out loud: her oaths are spoken, binding, and each one costs her something to mean. She glows faintly when it's working, leaking luminous vapor at the seams. The trait that should drive her card design is smaller than the flight — Windrunners bring others up with them. Where every other hero's power is measured in what she can do, Gale's is measured in what the units around her can suddenly do.

Modeled on: the Windrunners of Brandon Sanderson's Stormlight Archive.

Art traditionStorm chart with a luminous figure

Medium
Meteorological drafting — isobars, wind barbs, pressure gradients and storm-track projections on aged chart paper with fold creases and margin coordinates.
Light
The chart itself is flat and unlit. The figure is the only luminous thing on it, glowing from within and leaking bright vapor at the shoulders and heels.
Composition
Ground is drawn in the language of the map; the subject sits above the chart plane, out of its coordinate system. She is the one thing on the page the survey couldn't fix a position for.
Signature
One flight path traced across every card, entering one edge and leaving another — and where it passes an ally, that unit is drawn glowing too. The class is the only one whose art shows its power landing on someone else.
Palette
Chart buff · survey green · ink brown · storm grey · Stormlight white-gold
13-edgedancer

Prism

Edgedancer · 🟡🔵 Facet 💠 Locked

Frictionless. Nothing holds Prism, nothing slows her, nothing has ever successfully cornered her — she goes over and around and is simply elsewhere, and the precision reads as grace rather than calculation. Frost is the right color for it: ice is what the absence of friction looks like. But her defining trait is smaller and stranger than the mobility. She remembers the people nobody remembers — the servant, the extra, the name left off the list. In a cast of the cursed and the bargained-away, hers is the only power that cost nothing and the only oath that is simply about paying attention.

Modeled on: the Edgedancers of Brandon Sanderson's Stormlight Archive.

Art traditionFaceted crystalline geometry

Medium
Everything constructed from flat planes and hard vector edges — low-poly form language, but rendered with real refraction.
Light
Each facet carries a shifted, displaced copy of what sits behind it. Prismatic dispersion fringing every edge.
Composition
Strict geometric armature — golden-section or radial grid, visible if you look for it — cut by one long unbroken glide path. Every figure is mid-slide, never mid-step; no card in this class shows a planted foot.
Signature
The cleanest, most machined-looking cards in the game, with not one soft edge — and somewhere in each frame, one ordinary overlooked person rendered at the same fidelity as the hero. Nobody in an Edgedancer card is background.
Palette
Glacier white · cobalt · aurora teal · spectral fringe
14-soulcaster

Mirren

Soulcaster · 🟡🟣 Essence 🔮 Locked

Mirren doesn't destroy things — she argues with them until they agree to be something else. Stone into smoke, air into glass, a wall into not much at all. It is a negotiation rather than a blast, and she wins it because she is more certain of what the thing ought to be than the thing is. The cost runs in the other direction: it is slowly transmuting her. Crystal has grown along her left forearm and up under the jaw, and one eye has already gone to smoke. She is unhurried about this. She has done the arithmetic and finds the trade acceptable.

Modeled on: Soulcasting in Brandon Sanderson's Stormlight Archive — transmutation by persuasion, and the crystallization it costs the user.

Art traditionDark stained glass, mid-transmutation

Medium
Leaded came dividing every form into flat colored panes, darker and more saturated than church glass. The medium is the argument: glass is sand that was persuaded, by fire, to become light.
Light
From behind the image, always. The subject is a silhouette made of colored light, never lit from the viewer's side.
Composition
Each card catches one substance partway into becoming another — half the panel still stone, half already smoke, the boundary irregular and mid-negotiation.
Signature
The came doesn't just divide the image, it spreads — lead lines creeping across the hero herself like the crystal growing up her arm. In every card she is a little further along than she was in the last one.
Palette
Cobalt · amethyst · amber · lead grey
15-runeblade

Stave

Runeblade · 🟡⚪ Rune ᛞ Locked

Wrote the spell into the steel so the steel could remember it, and considers this obviously superior to remembering it himself. Stave is a smith first and a caster second and thinks the distinction is meaningless posturing. Methodical, literal-minded, quietly and enormously proud of work nobody will ever notice — the inside of a joint, the balance of a tang. His mechanical signature is deferred damage: the runes he carves keep going off after he has moved on. He doesn't win the exchange he is in, he wins the one three turns out, having set it up while you were watching something else.

Modeled on: the Runeblade class in Flesh and Blood — weapon attacks that seed accumulating arcane damage resolving on a delay.

Art traditionDamascened metal inlay

Medium
Pattern-welded steel — real watered-damascus swirl as the ground — with imagery inlaid in gold and silver wire.
Light
Specular and anisotropic. Highlights streak along the grain of the steel rather than pooling.
Composition
Knotwork borders that interlace with the subject, so figure and frame are one continuous line.
Signature
Rune channels cut into the metal and glowing from within — the only light in the class that isn't reflected.
Palette
Watered steel · gold inlay · silver · ember channel
16-shapeshifter

Pelt

Shapeshifter · 🟢🔵 Feral 🐾 Locked

Has been a person for so few of the last ten years that "Pelt" is a name other people use rather than one they answer to. Nature's adaptability plus frost's patience produces an ambush predator who waits out entire winters for a single opening. Communicates in roughly forty words total. Trusts animals immediately and people provisionally, and has never been given cause to revise either policy.

Art traditionSumi-e ink wash, single-breath brushwork

Medium
Ink on unbleached paper. One continuous loaded stroke per form — no correction, no second pass, visible dry-brush split at the stroke's end.
Light
None. Value is ink dilution and nothing else.
Composition
Enormous empty ground, subject placed hard off-center, weight in one corner.
Signature
The form never fully resolves — a human shoulder becomes an elk foreleg within the same stroke. You can't point to where the change happens.
Palette
Black ink · cold slate wash · raw paper · one vermilion seal
17-witch-doctor

Mire

Witch Doctor · 🟢🟣 Spore 🦠 Locked

Rot is just growth pointed the other way, and Mire has never understood why this upsets people. A healer whose methods everyone objects to right up until the moment they need them, with no bedside manner and a spotless success rate. Keeps things in jars. Labels the jars. Does not explain the labels.

Art traditionBio-culture bloom on batik

Medium
Actual growth patterns — mold colonies, bacterial culture, radial and fractal and irregular — laid over hand-dyed wax-resist cloth.
Light
Damp and ambient, like a cellar. Nothing casts a clean shadow because nothing has a clean edge.
Composition
Growth radiates outward from a point of infection somewhere in the frame. Composition is colonization.
Signature
Not one straight line anywhere in the class. Where every other style has an edge, this one has a bloom.
Palette
Swamp green · bruise purple · mud ochre · bone
18-ranger

Fletch

Ranger · 🟢⚪ Mark 🎯 Locked

The most competent ordinary person alive. No pact, no curse, no tragic backstory — Fletch simply out-prepared everyone else and finds the rest of the roster's suffering somewhat self-inflicted. Dry, self-sufficient, quietly amused. In a cast of people who paid terrible prices for power, she is the argument that you didn't have to.

Art traditionNaturalist field-journal gouache

Medium
Quick, confident gouache studies on tea-stained sketchbook paper. Pencil underdrawing left visible.
Light
Plain daylight, observed rather than composed. Nothing dramatized.
Composition
Several small studies per card rather than one hero image — three views of the same subject, a detail, a measurement.
Signature
Handwritten annotation and specimen labels in the margins, in the hero's own hand. The only class whose cards contain her notes as well as her image.
Palette
Paper tan · moss · umber · sky wash · red ink
19-lich

Rime

Lich · 🔵🟣 Web 🕸️ Locked

Chose to stop dying partway through the process and now lives permanently in the pause. Frost preserved the body; shadow keeps the mind running. Rime is unfailingly polite and completely without appetite — curious about the living the way one is curious about weather, with no stake in the outcome. Has all the time there is and no particular plan for it.

Art traditionFrost-fogged daguerreotype

Medium
Tintype photography — silver-halide bloom, heavy vignetting, chemical staining at the plate edges.
Light
Long-exposure studio light, harsh and frontal. Eyes never quite in focus because the sitter moved.
Composition
Formal portrait staging. Everyone posed, centered, and holding still far too long.
Signature
Spiderweb cracks in the emulsion radiating outward from the subject — the plate is failing where the figure touches it.
Palette
Silver · plate grey · tarnish violet · black
20-death-knight

Pall

Death Knight · 🔵⚪ Chill 🧊 Locked

Died in armor and got up still wearing it — raised by someone else's will, then freed of it later, which turned out to be the harder half. Pall keeps an oath he cannot remember taking and can no longer tell which of his choices were ever his. Slow, implacable, unbothered by damage. Speaks rarely and always finishes the sentence. Blue light where the eyes were, and a blade cold enough that carrying it is part of the sentence. Set him against the Runeblade deliberately: Stave's runes are authored, Pall's were inflicted — the same marks with opposite consent, and that is the entire distinction between the two classes.

Modeled on: World of Warcraft's frost-spec death knight.

Art traditionFunerary brass rubbing over frozen steel

Medium
Wax rubbing taken from a memorial brass — the flat, formal, full-length effigy with hands crossed and heraldry at the feet.
Light
None in the rubbing itself; all light comes from rime crystal blooming across the surface afterward.
Composition

What the inspirations change

Three of these names belong to someone else, and that is worth deciding about early rather than late.

Three names are Brandon Sanderson's

Windrunner, Edgedancer and Soulcaster are specific coinages from the Stormlight Archive — an ongoing, actively published series — not general fantasy vocabulary. That's fine for a project that stays yours. It becomes a real problem the moment this is sold, crowdfunded or published, and renaming twenty-one classes late is exactly the kind of thing that doesn't get done. If you want the option later, the concepts survive a rename cleanly: Skybinder, Slipdancer, Essencewright. The heroes — Gale, Prism, Mirren — are unaffected either way.

Runeblade, Death Knight and frost mage are a different case: all three long predate the specific games you're referencing them from, and are generic enough to use as-is.

One collision worth knowing: your Druid is named Briar with a Growth counter. Flesh and Blood's Briar, Warden of Thorns is a Runeblade hero built around growth mechanics. Since you're explicitly modeling your Runeblade on that class, having your Druid carry the name of theirs is going to confuse anyone who plays both. Briar is already in use, so this is a live rename decision rather than a hypothetical.

Hero names for Pyromancer, Druid, Sorceress and Necromancer are the ones already in use. The remaining seventeen follow the same convention: single ordinary English words that double as names. All 21 classes locked, 8 with a stated inspiration recorded.
