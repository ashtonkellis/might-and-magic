SUBJECT: A wooden training post, waist-thick and shoulder-high, worn smooth and
cut deep with notches where it has been struck ten thousand times -- the marks
crowded and overlapping near the middle where every strike lands, sparse and
tentative near the top where the early ones went wide. The wood is pale where it
is freshly cut and dark where old cuts have weathered. Dust is still hanging in
the air from the last strike. Behind it, small and thrown well out of focus, a
single figure in plain practice clothes is mid-motion, going again.

This card belongs to no class -- fourteen cards across seven of them hand out
Experience -- so it must not read as any one tradition. No magic, no glow, no
element, no class colour: it is the only card in the game whose subject is
simply work repeated until it changed the person doing it.

STYLE: polished photoreal digital fantasy art, not painting, with a shallow
depth of field that throws the background into soft blur and bokeh. Warm
neutral light from one side, late in the day, no visible source in frame. The
palette is wood, dust and worn steel -- ochre, umber, bone and a grey-green
patina, nothing saturated and no single hue that would claim it for an ink.
Mid-key: near-black under 2% of the image, the bulk of it in the midrange, so
the post reads as the dark shape against a lit ground.

FORMAT: landscape 3:2. Full bleed to all four edges. No text, lettering,
numerals, signatures, watermarks, borders, frames or card templates -- the card
frame is drawn separately and this image sits inside it. Keep nothing essential
within the outer 4% of the left and right edges.
